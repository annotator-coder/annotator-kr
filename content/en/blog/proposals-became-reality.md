There were three proposals I once put on the table.

"On the audio-based SNS, let's open a room on creator economy and engage with readers there."

"Let's try something that integrates surveys with content delivery — a StoryPoll. Looking at the data, there seems to be real demand for it."

"Let's produce short racing chart videos to tell our IT company growth histories."

All three happened.

---

Accepted proposals are rarer than you might think.

During the same period, plenty of others didn't go anywhere. The *bespoke* 3040 economics media proposal. *Know/edge*, a personalized service designed to fight algorithmic filter bubbles. The "Presidential Election User's Manual" I pitched directly to the early days of Publy. All of them exist only as documents now. Rejection became familiar — but each time something came back rejected, I went back with something else.

FactPL happened. I raised the idea of "a vertical newsletter on IT and startups" with the editor-in-chief — that was how it started. This was 2020, when almost nobody in Korea knew what a newsletter even was. I was a founding member involved from the design stage: format, homepage, content structure. It reached 20,000 subscribers with a 40% open rate. The biggest outcome of any proposal that went through.

---

Completion wasn't the end.

Making something always generated another proposal. Running FactPL, I kept feeling frustrated that readers only received. I wanted to know what they were curious about, where they were connecting emotionally. Integrating surveys with content seemed like it would work. Looking at the data, there was a clear reaction point. That became the StoryPoll proposal. It went through, ran, and readers participated.

When Clubhouse was blowing up in early 2021, I set up a creator economy room. Everyone was trying to figure out how to use the platform. That was the first time I understood what meeting readers through voice — rather than text — actually felt like.

The racing chart video came from the same logic. IT company growth histories visualized as competing, moving bars — they spread far better than static graphs.

---

Honestly, I don't know what the difference is between what worked and what didn't.

Sometimes it was timing. Sometimes it was whether there was someone to work with. Sometimes just luck. Writing the *bespoke* proposal gave me a much deeper understanding of what vertical media was — and that probably influenced the FactPL proposal later. Things that didn't work also accumulated somewhere, in ways I couldn't always see at the time.

What made it possible to keep making proposals was probably that rejection wasn't that frightening. A journalist calls people they've never met every single day and asks them questions. Rejection was just part of the routine.
