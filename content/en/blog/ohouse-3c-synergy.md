Whenever I get the chance to explain Ohouse to someone outside the company, I always get the same question: "Is it a commerce platform, or a community?" When I say both, they ask again: how is that possible?

3C (Content · Community · Commerce) sounds simple in name. Most platforms want all three — but doing all three well simultaneously is rare. Try to grow commerce and the community dies. Content piles up but never converts to purchases.

**Why Ohouse Was Different**

The starting point was different. It didn't begin as a selling platform. It started from the belief: "Wouldn't it be great if everyone could live in a better space?" — a place to share inspiration with each other. Content accumulated first. Community grew on top of that. Commerce appeared because users wanted it.

In fact, the store launched in 2016, two years after founding. For those two years, nothing was sold. When comments like "Where can I buy this?" started flooding in under other users' photos, the purchase function was finally added.

That sequence is everything. The community asked for commerce. Not the reverse.

**How the Flywheel Works**

The virtuous cycle that 3C creates operates like this: watching content → community forms → purchases happen within the community → buyers renovate their space and create new content → new communities form around similar purchases. As this cycle turns, data accumulates — better content becomes possible, recommendations become more precise.

Housewarming content symbolizes this perfectly. Over nine years, more than 9,000 housewarming posts have accumulated. If you visited one home every week, that would take 173 years. Even now, 140 new homes are posted each month, generating over 13 million views. These posts are not sales channels. They are sources of inspiration. Inspiration creates desire, and desire converts to purchases.

**The Core of Content Planning: Timing**

The question I thought about most in content planning was: "What does this person want right now?" The content needed by someone searching for their aesthetic, someone looking for a method, and someone needing reassurance are all different. What a single person and a newlywed couple want to see is different. Interests also shift with the seasons.

Good content doesn't begin with "We made this — please look at it." It begins with "What does this person need right now?" And it tracks results with data all the way to the end. Not finished once it's made — always tracking whether it was seen, whether there was a response, whether it led to a purchase.

**What "Customer Obsession" Really Means**

One of the seven values Ohouse emphasizes in team culture is "obsession with customers." It sounds simple, but applied in practice it means constantly asking uncomfortable questions: "What would a customer think?", "Wouldn't this be frustrating for a customer?", "Would I actually use this if I were a customer?"

The reason 3C could function organically was ultimately because every one of those questions started from that place. Content, community, commerce — all of it was a repetition of building with "if I were the customer" as the starting point, then validating with data.
