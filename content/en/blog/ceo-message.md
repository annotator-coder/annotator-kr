Is what the CEO says actually reaching the organization? Not many PR teams have a real answer to that question.

Most focus on delivery: issue a press release, air an internal broadcast, send an email to all staff. Message delivered. But did it land?

**The Measurement Problem**

CEO message effectiveness is hard to measure because the results are slow and dispersed. Stock price, hiring application rates, employee surveys, media mentions — isolating which of these moved because of the CEO's message, and proving causation, is nearly impossible.

So most teams either give up on measurement entirely, or settle for simple reach metrics like views and open rates. Outside consulting reports look polished but tend to be thin on actually executable insights.

**Voices from the Ground**

We're currently running a CEO message effectiveness validation project at the company. We've structured in-depth interviews and layered in anonymous surveys to build a more systematic approach.

It's not easy work. But to move beyond our existing inertia, we need to see the present state clearly. How messages are designed and delivered ultimately comes down to what the audience — the people receiving them — actually experience. You have to listen.

**The Path Forward**

The right answer is still emerging. But rather than groping around in the dark trying this and that, what may matter more is using AI assistance to build a more refined design framework, then analyzing the results with data.

PR is not just about crafting messages. It's also about designing how those messages are absorbed by the organization.

I'll post again once we have results.
